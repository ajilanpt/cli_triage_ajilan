# Interview — phase 10

## The case-level and run-level questions
Case-level: "is this specific test flaky?" — answered by observers 1-3, one test at a time.
Run-level: "should we stop this release?" — answered by fusion/the arbiter (phase 11),
looking at all of a build's failing tests together and producing one of the four outputs
from `design/01-decision-and-cost.md`. Conflating them means averaging away the one real
defect hiding among many flaky-looking failures in a large red build.

## The observed input overlap
Computed directly from each observer's `Reads` field: observers 2 (sequence) and 3
(retrieval) both derive from phase 05's TRUSTED-run gate over the same 3 raw `maven.log`
archives -- different representations (a pass/fail sequence vs. exception text) of the same
underlying observations, not two independent looks. Observer 1 (tabular) reads a separate
dataset (the FlakeFlagger CSV), computed by an entirely different process, and is genuinely
independent of the other two.

## How the contract prevents agreement from being mistaken for independent evidence
`ci_triage/contracts.py`'s `Evidence.source` field records which underlying data source
produced each piece of evidence, and `independent_sources()` counts distinct sources, not
records -- so observers 2 and 3 agreeing contributes one independent source to a count, not
two, even though two separate `Evidence` records exist. Combined with the mandatory
`calibrated` flag (no default; a probability without it cannot be constructed at all) and
`n_observations` (which separates "no evidence" from "evidence pointing everywhere," both of
which can otherwise present as a probability near 0.5), fusion in phase 11 has what it needs
to avoid inflating confidence from a correlated pair or trusting an unstated raw score as if
it were measured.
